# M4 — a guide that supports the cap before peeling loads the catch

**Historical proposal:** [M5](m5.md) now recovers interior mechanism space and
replaces the raised ribs with flat sides at the same maximum width. M4 was not
reported printed. The following investigation and deliverables remain evidence
for this earlier revision, not the current recommended sample.

**M4 replaced the unprinted M3 recommendation in this phase.** The visual
investigation exposed a missing load-path check. M3's smaller guide-only angle
was useful, but did not establish that its guide helped before a catch could be
bent out of capture. M4 changes that contact sequence in the checked geometry.
No new physical print or measured holding-force result is claimed.

Start with the [catch/receiver drawing](overlap_exploration/catch_before_guide.svg).
Blue is the real tooth, orange the cap, red a **blocked rigid ghost pose**, not
permitted free play. The revised cap meets the guide bearing while the catch
still has clearance. That physical distinction motivated the new geometry.

## What the drawings established

The user's concern was that an angle ratio alone did not explain whether the
change mattered physically. The earlier static SVG named parts, but did not show
movement, contact order or whether the supposed bearing pose was even possible
with the catches included. The corrective exploration is committed with its
[CAD experiment](../overlap_exploration.py), [diagonal check](../overlap_diagonal.py),
[drawing generator](../overlap_drawings.py), [pose/contact witnesses](overlap_exploration/contact_sequence.json)
and [diagonal receipt](overlap_exploration/diagonal_witness.json).

Three drawings answer distinct questions:

- [Case motion at a common scale](overlap_exploration/case_motion.svg) shows where
  the cap can be supported, the actual sideways displacement and a labelled 10×
  movement inset. The 12 mm panel is a controlled M3-catch variant, not an invented
  replacement for the printed M1 geometry.
- [Catch before guide](overlap_exploration/catch_before_guide.svg) magnifies the
  actual retaining faces equally. Red intersections identify cap poses that
  require deformation or disengagement. They cannot be reported as available
  rigid rocking of the closed product.
- [Overlap/clearance exploration](overlap_exploration/overlap_choice.svg) shows why
  increasing overlap alone did not resolve the weak route when the cap could
  also shift sideways. Its last row includes all M4 fit changes explicitly.

The motion rotates the cap about a declared near-side seam line. It checks
centered poses and then the cap shifted across its permitted side clearance
before peeling. Full body/guide and individual catches are intersected separately;
this identifies which load path obstructs motion first. Both principal rocking
planes and an additional combined diagonal path are challenged. This is finite
CAD exploration, not an unrestricted proof over every 3D pose or force equilibrium.

The old **3 cm M3** guide-only pose is reached only after one catch has begun
carrying the peel load. In the centered path it demands about **.385 mm** upward
movement at the far retaining face; allowing .25 mm of side movement increases
that demand to about **1.029 mm** before the opposing guide bearing engages.
The earlier local rotating-edge proxy can lose capture after only about
**.056–.157 mm** upward contact movement in its adverse lever-arm scenarios.
Those proxy values are assumptions, not measured print motion. Nevertheless,
the mismatch was large enough to reject the old rationale: the guide could arrive
far too late to protect the catch in a plausible weak route.

A preliminary 72 mm/.20 mm side-gap/.30 mm axial-play candidate looked adequate
when centered. Allowing side movement exposed about .114 mm axial demand before
bearing, exceeding the most adverse local proxy scenario. That rejected candidate
is retained as `M4_rejected_centered_only`; it prevents future agents from solving
the problem by silently fixing the cap's lateral position.

## The resulting revision

| Geometry | M3 | M4 | Physical purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Effective overlap | 30 mm | 78 mm | Bring bearing close to the cap end and establish the guide couple earlier |
| Nominal side clearance, each side | .25 mm | .15 mm | Reduce movement available before opposing guide contact |
| Nominal axial catch play | .25 mm | .40 mm | Allow the guide to take initial rocking rather than force the leaf to do it |
| Actual flat land per catch | 13.5 mm² | 14.4 mm² | Slightly larger capture from the reduced side gap; same 2 mm projection |
| Leaf thickness / length / tooth width | 2 / 32 / 9 mm | unchanged | Keep the previously investigated stiffness and release architecture |
| Assembled length / main width / depth | 264 / 75 / 51.2 mm | unchanged | Preserve whole-case size |
| Local maximum width | 77 mm | unchanged | Historical exact 74.2 mm criterion remains FAIL; no waiver implied |

This trades a longer insertion/withdrawal passage and .15 mm more axial take-up
for substantially earlier guide support and less lateral freedom. The axial play
is not hidden or described as zero wobble. The sleeve now extends near the cap
end, where it can support the shell rather than leaving the long cap wall to
transfer the load through the catches. The narrower side gap remains an
uncalibrated PETG fit hypothesis, to be checked with the matching sample.

In the **nominal shifted-cap path**, M4's guide bearing obstructs rocking at
about **.222°**, while the catch would not pick up until about **.311°** if the
guide were omitted. At guide contact the retaining face still has about
**.114 mm axial clearance**. The nominal depth-rocking and diagonal cases also
put guide contact before catch load. This is the meaningful change, rather than
a degree ratio by itself. The later hypothetical catch event is not claimed to
be reachable through a perfectly rigid guide.

Nine declared geometry variations use .10/.15/.20 mm side clearances and
.35/.40/.45 mm axial play, with the full side movement allowed for each geometry.
Eight still put the bearing before significant catch demand. The adverse
.20 mm side-gap/.35 mm axial-play corner loads a catch slightly earlier: about
**.031 mm** axial demand, below the least favorable approximately **.056 mm**
local proxy capture-loss movement. Combined diagonal rocking gives a similar
**.0305 mm** demand in that adverse case. These are limited dimensional and
contact-hypothesis checks, not a calibrated distribution of actual printed errors
or a rigorous lower bound on pull-off strength. Larger errors or softer/weak
printed walls can still defeat the mechanism.

The guide can therefore plausibly take the bending duty early, while the catches
retain against axial withdrawal. Straight-pull resistance still involves the
cantilevers and receivers; guide-first geometry is not an axial holding-force
measurement, impact rating, printed fatigue result or proof against every peel path.
The shell must still supply real stiffness once contact occurs.

## Contents, operation and the matching test

[Current CAD checks](m4/cad.json) cover 277 full/sample poses, both real lands,
root/receiver preservation, intentional squeeze and withdrawal, forbidden motion
outside the guide clearance, individual brush/tube passage and handled end rounds.
The toothbrush and tube sweeps cover the whole extended mouth continuously, not
just the old 30 mm region. Actual combined easy packing and prolonged insertion
drag remain physical unknowns. Liner accessories remain outside scope.

Close by pushing the cap fully home and allowing both controls to return. Open
with the joint unloaded: press both controls and pull until the cap mouth clears
the teeth (10 mm is checked), then let the controls return and slide the rest of
the guide out. The checks include that return while the long guide is still engaged.

The test body is **113.6 mm** high; its **90 mm cap is the complete production cap**,
verified identical by CAD. The assembled sample is **125.6 mm** long. It preserves
both complete roots, 12 mm below the roots, the entire 78 mm sleeve, receiving
band, cap roof and print direction. The cap can be reused with a later full body.
Only the long lower body and contents are omitted, so the sample still does not
reproduce the complete body's compliance, remote grips or loaded travel behavior.

Before a full-body print, use the sample to compare closing/return, unpressed
pulling and gentle rocking, and deliberate release. A click or button return alone
is insufficient: if the cap separates similarly without operating the controls,
the test has found inadequate retention. Binding, whitening, incomplete return or
damage calls for correction before the complete body. A successful specimen supports
its represented joint; it does not turn the earlier M1 coupon experience into
full-product qualification. This sample is cheaper than the full body while keeping
the actual long guide and full-cap load path, and avoids throwing away the cap.

## Computation and manufacturing boundaries

No new large contact solver or native framework was developed. CAD uses CadQuery
2.8.0/OpenCASCADE; contact onset is bracketed to .0005° with a 1e-5 mm³ Boolean
intersection discriminator. That is numerical discrimination, not print accuracy.
The local elastica/receiver proxy is inherited from the documented M3 study and
remains uncalibrated. SVGs embed source/data hashes so changed source cannot silently
reuse an old visual proof. Useful negative cases and the blocked ghost drawings
are retained alongside the successful current path.

M4's isolated leaf/pad shape is the M3 shape translated outward .10 mm; the current
CAD checks establish exact equivalence. The source-associated old 7 N/E1200 pad
traction result therefore transfers only to that same isolated local pressure
fixture: at least 2.124 mm tooth retraction, with mesh/material/recovery limitations
retained. The actual retaining load patch is now slightly wider, so the old 12 N
traction displacement is not relabelled as a new M4 solve. Full-cap release contact,
printed comfort and strain/recovery acceptance remain separate unknowns.

The full body prints **252 mm** high and cap **90 mm**, within the selected QIDI Q2C
256 mm height; the sample body is 113.6 mm. Both use the same upright body/inverted
cap directions and paired layout. This is a tall-body tradeoff with 4 mm nominal
height remaining, not a larger assembled case. The primary PETG diagnostic setup
uses a .4 mm nozzle, .2 mm layers, two walls and 7% adaptive cubic. Bed contact and
rounded feet are preserved; the sample body starts on its annular cut face.
Layer strength, tall-part straightness and fit remain physical properties, not
consequences of a solid-CAD modulus.

Current full/sample STEP and STL are published from the same source version.
[Full slice](m4/case_export_slice.json), [sample slice](m4/joint_export_slice.json),
[full toolpath review](m4/case_toolpaths.svg), [sample toolpath review](m4/joint_toolpaths.svg)
and [publication association](m4/publication.json) retain the selected-profile
manufacturing evidence. Slice acceptance and support-probe inspection do not
establish physical bridge quality or Orca GUI STEP import behavior.

Both primary slices complete without notices. Their actual cap bridges reach
about 13.06 mm at Z86.6; the body tooth bridge reaches 7.31 mm at Z178.2 in the
full body and Z39.8 in the crop. The support probe proposes trees inside the cap,
with interfaces near its floor (Z2.4–2.6) and retaining ledges (Z86.0–86.2).
These locations were inspected headlessly. The primary layout uses no supports;
printed bridge finish remains unknown, and support removal must not be presumed
harmless to the retaining surfaces.


For toolpath inspection, retain each slice with `--slice-keep-run` and run
`./execute.py model/dental_travel_case/m4_toolpaths.py case RETAINED_DIRECTORY`
(use `joint` for the sample). This reuses the existing toolpath reader.
The current verification composes CAD, visual evidence, transferred local operation,
full/sample slice and physical unknowns; it has no global score or automatic promotion.

The delivered revision passes 277 CAD checks and 135 relevant regression tests
(`test_dental_visual_load_path`, `test_dental_retention_proxy`,
`test_dental_retention` and `test_product_verification_entrypoints`). The
[current verification report](m4/verification.json) retains the historical width
criterion FAIL and physical UNKNOWNs alongside the successful computational checks.

Final independent engineering review inspected the actual source, three exploration
drawings, principal/diagonal contact witnesses, full/sample publication associations
and slicing evidence. It found no consequential correction needed for the next
prototype. Its conclusion is conditional on printed fit, guide/shell stiffness,
bridge finish and elastic recovery; it does not establish a travel-retention rating.

The design guidance now requires motion at common scale, actual gaps/capture,
first-contact order, explicit blocked poses and relevant side freedom before
claiming that a numerical improvement protects the mechanism. It also explicitly
requires committing useful exploration drawings/generators and regenerating
current test pieces with model changes. A regression changes one shared production
source and checks that **both** full and coupon publications become stale.
