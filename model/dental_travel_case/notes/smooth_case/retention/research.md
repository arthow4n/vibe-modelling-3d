# Research used for the C retention decision

[Covestro, Snap-Fit Joints for Plastics: A Design Guide](https://solutions.covestro.com/-/media/covestro/solution-center/brands/downloads/imported/1557218421.pdf), pp. 14–15 and 24: separation force uses the return-ramp angle and friction as well as transverse deflection force. Both flexible mating pieces share the required movement, so treating the cap as rigid overstates resistance. Surface condition affects friction. These principles support summing the actual body/cap radial compliances and deriving axial resistance from the actual withdrawal energy; they do not supply a travel-box force requirement. No material/friction table is treated as calibration of this printed PETG joint.

[Polymaker, PolyLite PETG product information V1.1](https://polymaker.com/wp-content/tech-docs/PolyLite_PETG_PIS_EN_V1.1.pdf), p. 6, gives example specimen values of 1472 ± 270 MPa tensile modulus and 1174 ± 64 MPa bending modulus. These make the retained 1200 MPa comparison hypothesis plausible as a force-scale assumption, not a measurement of this spool, wall layout or layer bonding. The broad 900–2200 MPa scenarios deliberately expose sensitivity; they are not confidence bounds or a guaranteed printed range.

There is no universal useful pull-off force established by these sources. The prior 8–18 N centered-pull design target is retained as agent judgment: roughly the steady force of supporting 0.8–1.8 kg, convenient for discussing magnitude rather than a hand-comfort or safe-payload rating. For an illustrative 0.3 kg unsupported loaded body, gravity alone supplies about 2.9 N, before any jolt. The ideal energy threshold matters alongside the slow-pull peak because axial free play permits acceleration before catch loading. Real loaded mass, bag motion, wet grip, friction, damping and off-center peel remain unmeasured; the physical trial must test those relationships rather than calling any static force a bag-impact pass.
