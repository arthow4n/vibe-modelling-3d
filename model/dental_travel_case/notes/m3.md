# M3 — practical retention prototype

**Current decision:** deliver M3 as the next computationally screened prototype.
It has a credible improvement over the failed printed M1 without requiring an
intractable full-shell contact solve. This is a prototype decision, not a measured
backpack retention rating. No new print has been made or requested.

The user requested this continuation after the [M2 investigation](m2.md): adapt
the analysis to available resources, explain the joint with cross-section SVGs,
exclude liner compatibility, deliver a new model and reserve independent review
for the completed implementation. Liner accessories are outside this phase.
The original M1 source, exports and authoritative printed failure remain intact.

## The practical change

M3 uses the existing two integral printable parts. Relative to printed M1:

| Feature | M1 | M3 | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Guide overlap | 12 mm | 30 mm | Reduce bearing force for the same rocking moment; maintain alignment |
| Leaf thickness / length | 1.6 / 40.4 mm | 2 / 32 mm | Reduce inward bending under eccentric opening load |
| Flat retaining land, each catch | 1.5 × 8 mm | 1.5 × 9 mm | Preserve real positive capture with wider bearing |
| Tooth/pocket exterior clearance | .15 mm | .45 mm | Avoid consuming return margin at .25 mm guide offset |
| Pocket exterior skin including reinforcement | .8 mm | 1.5 mm | Reduce receiver spreading in the declared strip proxy |
| Main / local maximum width | 74.2 / 74.2 mm | 75 / 77 mm | Preserve contents clearance despite thicker leaves; strengthen receiver |
| Overall length / depth | 264 / 51.2 mm | unchanged | Preserve principal use envelope |

The first 74.2 mm-wide thicker-leaf candidate left only .45 mm clearance to the
established individual toothpaste proxy, below the existing .8 mm clearance
screen. It was rejected. Moving each side out .4 mm restores that clearance
without reducing release travel or the backing-channel reserve. The resulting
77 mm maximum width explicitly fails the earlier exact 74.2 mm criterion; this
is a visible prototype compromise, not a user waiver. The case is still 22.2 mm
narrower at its largest local feature than the earlier 99.2 mm case.

M3 preserves R4 handled ends, the 2.7 mm release envelope and raised M2B controls.
It does not inherit a liner. Individual brush and tube proxies clear; combined
real contents were not resimulated as invented box packing. The printed M1 fit
observation remains relevant historical evidence, not proof of effortless M3 use.

## Why the simpler simulation is informative

The previous study demonstrated inward catch bending under axial force but used
a force fixed in the world Z direction. That can miss a feedback route:

1. An axial pull acts outside the leaf's neutral axis and bends it inward.
2. Its lower retaining face rotates with the tooth.
3. If the receiver edge contacts that rotated face, the normal reaction can also
   point inward, increasing retraction. Receiver retreat consumes more capture.

That is a plausible weak route in M1, not a claim that the exact microscopic
printed escape has now been measured. A horizontal receiver face may instead
sustain a more nearly axial reaction. The analysis keeps these **different contact
hypotheses** separate. Neither is guaranteed to bound all actual corner contact,
friction, distributed contact, shell twisting or post-disengagement motion.

[The product-local proxy](../retention_proxy.py) uses a planar finite-rotation
Euler–Bernoulli elastica, a rigid eccentric tooth and a frictionless reaction.
The axial force is tensile, so its straightening effect is retained. It solves
force/moment equilibrium with either fixed-global axial force or a reaction
following the rotating tooth face. It measures the inward movement of the outer
flat-land edge, its projected remaining capture and the radial force.
Receiver retreat is a simply supported/fixed-end strip sensitivity, not a fitted
spring measured from M1. Contact-point lever arms cover the inner/outer land edges
in separate controls. The model stops at loss of the useful flat land; it does
not manufacture a full pull-off curve or disengagement work after that event.

The independent fixed-world response check is useful: the beam was derived from
CAD dimensions, with no fitted modulus or correction multiplier, and agrees with
actual-CAD local FEM to roughly 6–9% in shoulder displacement. That supports the
local bending reduction. It does **not** validate the rotating-edge hypothesis or
qualify the receiver/guide strip springs.

## Comparison and sensitivity

All new native models use CalculiX 2.21 with Gmsh quadratic tetrahedra, geometric
nonlinearity and uncalibrated isotropic PETG E1200 MPa, Poisson ratio .38. The root
stub is the same 1 mm fixed region used in the fair M1/M2B comparison. Axial force
is integrated over the actual 13.5 mm² M3 land. Pad traction permits rotation.
No printed strength threshold or universal 1% acceptance is adopted.

| Quantity / declared condition | Failed M1 | M2B | M3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Actual-CAD mean inward shoulder, fixed-world 12 N, E1200, fixed 1 mm root stub | 1.046 mm | .758 mm | .531 mm |
| Independent fixed-world elastica estimate, same force/modulus | 1.104 mm | .822 mm | .577 mm |
| Proxy first flat-capture loss, E1200, .25 mm fit loss, simply supported pocket, center lever arm, **N per catch** | 4.30 | 7.77 | 13.69 |
| Proxy adverse setting, E800, .5 mm fit loss, simply supported pocket, center arm, **N per catch** | 2.43 | 4.38 | 7.70 |
| Proxy adverse outer-edge lever arm, **N per catch** | 1.85 | 3.31 | 5.97 |
| Qualified full-product pull-off force / work | Not established | Not established | Not established |

The native M3 axial displacement is **49.3% smaller than M1** in the same local
fixture. These are actual structural results, not a binary overlap comparison.
The proxy's force values are conditional **loss-of-flat-capture loads**, not
measured holding forces or guaranteed lower bounds. Symmetric equal sharing would
put twice that force on the complete cap; one remaining catch bears the whole
axial force and requires an additional guide couple. Those relationships must
not be silently treated as equivalent fixtures.

The declared comparison varies E800/1200/2200 MPa, radial capture loss 0/.25/.5 mm,
and pocket-strip end fixity 1–4. All 18 paired settings improve from M1 to M3;
one stiff M3 setting has no crossing by the declared 30 N/catch search ceiling
(recorded null, not infinite strength). Across that grid the M1 first-loss range
is 2.43–10.12 N/catch; M3 starts at 7.70 N/catch and has one result above 30.
These are hypothetical material/fit ranges, not a distribution of actual prints.
Changing the contact lever arm materially changes the force prediction; the
separate edge controls make this model uncertainty visible.

### Rocking and off-center loading

A bearing couple requires approximately M / overlap at each end of the guide.
At .1 N·m, that is 8.33 N for M1 and 3.33 N for M3. This load reduction is independent
of the unknown PETG modulus. Joint angular freedom and elastic bearing rotation
are different: the earlier exact guide-only CAD screen gives roughly 1.4° versus
.5° at .9 mm lift; it is a prescribed pose with catches omitted, not loaded stiffness.
M3 repeats the CAD screen on its actual geometry in `cad.json`.

The guide spring sensitivity models cap wall strips between rounded corners and
two shorter body bearing pads supported by the integral liner-side webs. The
webs are structural body features, unrelated to the excluded removable liner
accessory. It varies effective axial load spreading from one-quarter to one-half
of overlap and end fixity by four. This is an interpretable compliance scenario,
not a calibrated shell substitute. The internal supporting webs justify the
shorter body span; treating the entire end wall as unsupported would miss them.

[Handling scenarios](m3/proxy.json) cover 5/10/20 N axial force and
0/.1/.25 N·m moment: catch-carried moment, guide-carried moment, and one lost catch.
A .1–.25 N·m moment corresponds to roughly 1.1–2.8 N transverse force 90 mm from
the seam. This is a modest declared handling range, not a standard or user-approved
acceptance requirement. No material properties were tuned to the printed failure.
At E1200, .5 mm fit loss, 10 N axial plus .1 N·m carried by the guide, M3 retains
.342 mm in this scenario; M2B is just below zero and M1 is outside the strip's
small-bending scope. At .25 N·m the same M3 scenario also loses capture. Low-E,
poor load spreading and one-catch cases remain concerning. Thus the study does
not certify arbitrary backpack loading: it exposes where a practical prototype
should be challenged rather than reporting one favorable force as a rating.

The linear guide-strip estimate is flagged outside its scope when relative
bearing deflection exceeds half the thinner wall. Such a result is unresolved,
not a calculated physical failure. Alternate load paths are explicitly separated;
the study does not assign the same whole moment to both catches and guides.
Local guide movement is conservatively added to the radial capture budget in a
scenario, not asserted as a rigorous full-assembly bound.

### Intended release and numerical quality

The actual local M3 leaf under 7 N pad force at E1200 retracts every selected
tooth point at least 2.124 mm; pad mean travel is 1.298 mm. This clears the 2 mm
combined radial disengagement allowance used for the declared adverse receiver
position. It omits production-root blend and cap contact. Peak principal strain
is 1.071%, reported without a strength/recovery acceptance. The simple beam predicts
4.70–12.92 N per pad across E800–2200 for the prescribed 2.7 mm terminal travel.
Finger comfort, fatigue, creep and anisotropic printed recovery remain unmeasured;
M3 deliberately trades higher squeeze effort for less opening compliance.

A uniform .85 mm local mesh failed Gmsh high-order ScaledJac optimization, before
mechanics. One adaptation to curvature sizing (maximum 1.2 mm, minimum .35 mm,
12 points/full circle) succeeded. Mean shoulder displacement changes .16% from
the preliminary uniform 1.2 mm local mesh. The latter used the rejected narrow
body's identical catch: saved native BREPs are checked after its -.4 mm rigid
translation to establish exact geometry equivalence. Peak strain changes about
11%, so it remains mesh-sensitive. No further solver integration was pursued.
Force imbalance stays below 1.1e-7 relative in the curved traction run and below
1e-6 in the pressure run. Raw settings, native identity, mesh and failure logs are
retained through the existing physical-analysis evidence conventions.

Reduced-model controls cover the small-load beam limit, zero eccentricity,
fixed versus rotating reaction direction, receiver weakening, lost engagement,
fit loss, thicker leaves, guide length, mesh/tolerance refinement, and independent
actual-CAD fixed-world FEM comparison. Numerical failure propagates; loads above
the first loss are not forced through a post-loss equilibrium solve.

## Manufacturing decision

The delivered STEP/STL share the current layout: body upright (204 mm), cap
closed-end down (90 mm), side by side. OrcaSlicer 2.4.2 accepted the layout within
its QIDI Q2C 270 × 270 × 256 mm profile. Diagnostic setup: .4 mm nozzle, .2 mm
layers, PETG, two walls, 7% adaptive cubic. These settings are not a calibrated
mechanical material model. The solid-model local leaf results presume effective
load-bearing stock; verify actual leaf paths and use appropriate wall settings
when preparing a physical print. Z-oriented leaf roots remain layer-sensitive.

The smoke slice completed without notices; its automatic-support probe generated
trees inside the cap. [Actual path inspection](m3/toolpaths.svg) localizes interface
layers near the cap floor at Z2.4 and pocket ledges at Z86; the trees occupy the
interior between them. The support-free primary
slice bridges the receiving ledges at Z86.4 and the body tooth at Z178.2. The cap
bridge paths span about 13.2 mm between their anchors; those bridges, their
attachment and their finish require a real print assessment. The bridge is not
a disconnected tooth/shelf island. M3's targeted first-feature CAD sections remain
connected. The former M2A islands are not reintroduced.

This inspection resolves the location of the support signal, **not** the actual
support-free printing requirement. The primary slice is a usable support-free
prototype layout; bridge quality is unknown. Automatic trees can occupy the deep
cap cavity and contact retention surfaces, so their removal should not be assumed
easy or dimensionally harmless. No new manufacturing qualification is invented.
An STL slice does not establish Orca GUI STEP import behavior.

## Reproduce and review

From the repository root:

```sh
./execute.py --threads 1 model/dental_travel_case/retention_proxy.py
./execute.py --threads 2 model/dental_travel_case/m3_checks.py
./execute.py --threads 1 model/dental_travel_case/m3_explain.py
./execute.py --threads 1 model/dental_travel_case/retention_structure.py eccentric --variant m3 --modulus 1200 --mesh 1.2 --mesh-min-size .35 --mesh-curvature-points 12 --load 12 --receiving-land --root-stub --destination /tmp/dental_m3_land_12_curved
./execute.py --threads 1 model/dental_travel_case/retention_structure.py press_force --variant m3 --modulus 1200 --mesh 1.2 --load 7 --destination /tmp/dental_m3_press_7
./evaluate_model.py model/dental_travel_case/m3_case.py --views none --slice --report model/dental_travel_case/notes/m3/export_slice.json --summary
./execute.py model/dental_travel_case/verification.py --variant slim-m3
uv run --locked pytest -q tests/test_dental_retention_proxy.py tests/test_dental_retention.py tests/test_product_verification_entrypoints.py
```

Use `--slice-keep-run` when regenerating toolpath inspection, pass its retained
directory to `m3_toolpaths.py`, and run `m3_evidence.py` only after native/publication
completion with geometry sources frozen. Native execution uses the existing
coordinator/isolation; the reduced simulation needs no new solver or scheduler.
Source-associated verification separates CAD, reduced hypotheses, local FEM,
slicing and physical unknowns. The retained M1 failure never becomes an M3 print.

Final verification covers 217 CAD poses and 130 passing focused regression tests.
The [composed verification report](m3/verification.json) preserves the exact-width
FAIL and physical UNKNOWNs alongside scoped CAD/proxy/local-FEM/slice passes.

One final independent review inspected the completed implementation, actual CAD,
SVGs, native results, source/export hashes and scoped interpretation. It found no
consequential defect requiring correction and accepted a computationally screened
prototype handoff. It explicitly rejected interpreting proxy capture-loss loads
as actual holding force or the slice as support-free physical qualification. The
reviewer ran no studies and requested no further full-contact solve.
