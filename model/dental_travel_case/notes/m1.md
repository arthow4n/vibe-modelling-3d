# M1 — narrower case with catches at both widthwise ends

Current revision: **264 × 74.2 × 51.2 mm**, exactly 25 mm narrower than the first
M1. Two PETG cups, hidden hooks at opposite end walls and no item insert.
**Physical status:** full box printed; usable for its basic storage task, but the
joint feels insufficiently stable and the catches can release by pulling the
halves apart without pressing the controls. Secure backpack retention is not
accepted. Contents fit with effort, toothpaste flat tail down/cap up, with room
for one floss case. **Development stopped at the user's request: no further
revision or print is planned.** Geometry and exports remain unchanged.

## Full-box print feedback — 2026-10-15

The user reports printing the full slim box and says it does its job. Attribution
to the current narrow M1 at `001b739` is inferred from conversation; the exact
printed artifact/hash, material, printer, orientation and slicer settings were
not supplied. Do not infer them from the diagnostic slice or older clip print.

Observed results:

- The brush and toothpaste fit, although the user had to force them in. The
  toothpaste was inverted: **flat sealed tail downward, cap upward**. One floss
  case also fits. This establishes this user's actual combination, not easy
  loading, unsqueezed toothpaste, alternative tube fit or a new required floss slot.
- The joint feels too short and insufficiently stable. The user worries a lateral
  hit could break it; **no actual side-impact break or load measurement was
  reported**. The nominal CAD sleeve overlap is 12 mm.
- Small printed samples seemed to hold, but the full box's bite is inadequate:
  pulling the two halves apart releases it **without pressing either cantilever
  release**. This fails the intended secure retention despite nominal tooth
  engagement and prior digital checks. The exact elastic escape path is unknown.
- The user suggests roughly **two to three times the overlap** (about 24–36 mm
  relative to the nominal 12 mm), with uncertainty about the amount. This is an
  untested direction for future work, not a validated length or universal ratio.
- The user does not intend another print and explicitly requests lessons only,
  **no revision**. Keep the usable existing object and stop development here.

The important gap was whole-shell behavior. Rigid contact checks showed catches
behind ledges, and the beam screen addressed deliberate leaf motion. Neither
established resistance to opening through flexible cup walls, receiver spreading,
peeling or rocking at the short overlap. Those are plausible escape mechanisms,
not diagnosed causes of this print. The cropped joint omitted long-wall behavior
and complete-object leverage; its apparent success did not transfer to secure
full-box retention. A longer overlap could improve guidance and load transfer,
but would still need assessment of root sections, receiver stiffness, loading
access and accidental release. Deeper teeth alone are not a demonstrated cure.

For future enclosures, review seam bearing span and lateral-hit leverage before
finalizing overlap, and challenge the complete closure with off-center pull,
peel/rock and shell-spreading escape routes without operating the release. Use a
representative full-shell restraint/span when those routes matter; label a local
coupon as local evidence. No new analysis or experiment is authorized here.

The [saved verification receipt](m1_verification.json) and current verification
plan are **pre-print digital evidence**, not a current physical acceptance report.
Their physical UNKNOWNs are superseded within the scope above by this user report:
basic use is observed, retention is inadequate, joint stability is unsatisfactory,
and impact strength, force, life, button safety and end comfort remain unmeasured.
No CAD, slice or numerical rerun is needed for this documentation-only update.

## User instruction and architecture

The user selected M1 on 2026-10-14: a minimal separate-cap case, no exterior hinge,
a rounded overlap and resistance to popping open when struck in a backpack.
The 2026-10-15 print feedback authorizes 25 mm less width, deeper/longer teeth,
retention at both widthwise ends and much larger top/bottom fillets. Inserts are deferred; floss needs no volume.
The attached 250–255 mm brush, approximately 30 mm handle, and full 160 × 55 × 40 mm
measured tube remain the contents basis. Room for alternative tubes remains open.

The existing two-cup architecture is retained. The body shoulder seats the cap;
a 12 mm nonround sleeve guides alignment and transfers lateral bearing loads.
Two integral body leaves retain the cap in blind pockets, now at opposite X ends
instead of both near the left side. Without the hooks, the two cups still guide,
seat and stop coherently. No exterior collar, hinge, hardware or loose release
parts are added. Local liners behind the leaves protect the cavity from direct
finger contact; these are closure packaging rather than item organizers.

One hand pinches the two recessed **end-wall** contacts below the cap while the
other pulls the cap lengthwise. Integral shallow pads reduce the reach into the
ports. The grip now spans 74.2 mm; actual hand comfort remains a physical test.
For closing, hold both contacts in, push the cap to its hard stop, then release.
Entry ramps remain available for a gentle passive snap-on trial after this route
works. No passive snap-on force or durability has been measured.

## Packing and the lesson from the first print

The first layout used an aligned solid 55 × 40 mm tube proxy beside the 30 mm
handle. This was a conservative envelope, and did not explore rotated packing,
longitudinal staggering or the varying tube profile before calling the case slim.
The user's feedback correctly reopens that representation choice.

The revision follows the requested 25 mm reduction. Its ordinary body cavity is
70.2 × 47.2 mm. The sleeve opening is approximately **65.1 × 42.1 mm**, with local
lined channels at the two ends. A centered 55 × 40 mm rectangular tube envelope
passes this complete opening individually with the declared >=0.8 mm CAD clearance.
The 30 mm handle also passes individually. This does not establish that both items
can occupy or traverse the case together in the same pose.

The user has physically tried the actual brush and toothpaste together and
confirms that the diagonal arrangement works. This supersedes the earlier lack
of a packing description; no further orientation measurements are requested.
The current plan records that confirmation as **physical PASS for the arrangement**,
rather than inventing a tapered CAD tube and claiming a digital packing proof.
The later full-box trial confirms actual loading only with effort and the
toothpaste tail down/cap up; it does not qualify alternative tubes or easy access.
The brush-only CAD view does not contradict or replace the user-confirmed
toothpaste placement.

The general lesson is saved in [method selection](../../../.codex/skills/engineering-method-selection/SKILL.md#refine-switch-combine-or-remain-inconclusive),
with an early route from the design concept screen: explore allowed rotations and
staggering, distinguish conservative bounds from a minimum, and use measured
profiles or physical trials when section shape matters. Do not infer a thinner
tube solely because it makes the desired case pass.

## Retained print setup and original trial procedure

Proposed setup remains **PETG, 0.4 mm nozzle / 0.2 mm layers**. Keep the delivered
orientations: body butt down and cap exterior top down. The cropped test rings
stand on flat annular ends. Use the checked 3 mm brim. Both layouts fit the Q2C
270 × 270 × 256 mm envelope. Do not scale either half independently.
The large end radius is tangent to a 45-degree foot rather than starting as a full
quarter-circle at the bed. This limits outward growth above the first layer while
keeping visibly softer ends. Internal R2 blends preserve wall stock behind R4.

Ordinary cup walls/end panels are 2 mm; sleeve and leaf sections 1.6 mm; leaf-side
slots 0.8 mm; receiving band 2.7 mm and blind-pocket skins 0.8 mm. Inward liner ramps
are supported slopes. Gabled control-port roofs close gradually; pocket ledges
span 8.4 mm, with 2 mm tooth projections and sloped finger pads. The initial flat
control-port roof triggered automatic supports beneath that bridge; it was replaced
with the gabled roof before final delivery. Exterior seam/port
edges are softened without rounding away the functional tooth shoulders. The leaves
run along print Z: layer bonding, recovery and deposited retaining ledges require
physical qualification beyond the reported inadequate full-box holding.

Headless smoke reviews use OrcaSlicer 2.4.2 and the named Q2C/Generic PETG profiles
with the local [3 mm brim diagnostic process](slicer_review/process_3mm_brim.json),
two walls and 7% adaptive cubic. Profile temperatures, flow and speeds are diagnostic,
not a calibrated production job. Receipts: [full](slicer_review/m1_case.json) and
[test](slicer_review/m1_joint_test.json). Matching STL acceptance does not qualify
Orca's separate GUI STEP import. No printer job is sent by this workflow.

Both final layouts passed the headless slice with no notices and **no generated
supports** in the automatic-support probe. The [actual joint toolpaths](../renders/tests/m1/m1_toolpaths.svg)
show the finger pad, gabled port crown, retaining ledge and pocket roof. Short
bridge/overhang paths remain at the retaining features; slicer acceptance does
not establish their deposited strength. The two-wall receiver band contains some
infill; these toolpaths do not explain or overturn the reported inadequate
full-box holding. The actual print settings are unknown.
Estimated full-layout use is **167.64 g / 4 h 38 min**; the revised joint pair is
**50.55 g / 1 h 37 min**, including the selected brim. These are slicer estimates.

The source-association guard was also challenged with missing association, a stale
builder digest and a mismatched paired STEP: all were correctly rejected, while
the current native export association was accepted. All ten digital checks pass;
the saved pre-print report remains unresolved. The later physical findings above
are authoritative for current use and retention status.

The following is the original trial procedure, retained for provenance, **not a
request for another print**. Development is stopped.

1. Print both revised test halves and use your confirmed diagonal arrangement;
   no further orientation description is required. The ring primarily checks the
   revised closure and its tighter opening; it omits the full case's long walls.
2. Check comfortable access to both **end-wall** contacts. Squeeze both, seat the
   cap completely, then release. Confirm both hooks recover into their pockets.
3. Try pulling at either end and off-center without pressing, then with just one
   end pressed. It should stay caught. Press both and pull: it should release
   without cracking or whitening. Do not infer a loaded carrying rating from this.
4. Repeat and check recovery after a closed dwell. Record slipping, binding,
   difficult finger reach, excessive play or poor ledge/skin printing.
5. Try the full prototype only after the narrower packing and closure are useful.
   Keep the cavity plain; any handle insert remains deferred by the user.
