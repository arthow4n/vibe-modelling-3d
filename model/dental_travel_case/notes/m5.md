# M5 — reclaim the unused catch channels, keep the long guide

**Current space-recovery prototype; unprinted.** The user rejects excessive
interior mechanism bulk in unprinted M4, keeps the flat-exterior preference and
authorizes autonomous alternatives and a reasonable new variant. M5 recovers
about **33 mL / 4% overall**, with about **10% more central area in the long guide
region**. This is useful local recovery, not a dramatic increase in whole-case
capacity. The catch-level entrance remains the limiting passage.

Start with the [actual interior sections](m5/interior_sections.svg) and
[longitudinal guard drawing](m5/guard_length.svg). Blue is body/guide, orange is
cap and green is the central storage space. The drawings and their
[generator](../m5_drawings.py) are committed with the investigation.

The flat exterior is **77 × 51.2 × 264 mm**, with the existing rounded corners
and R4 handled ends. M4's broad body was 75 mm wide and its raised catch ribs
reached 77 mm. M5 makes the sides flat at that previous maximum: **the broad
body grows 2 mm**, while the maximum width, depth and assembled length stay the
same. This is an explicit compromise, not free space recovered entirely by
removing material. The historical exact 74.2 mm width criterion remains FAIL.

Both parts and the test pair are new M5 geometry. Do not mix M4/M5 cap/body or
assume liner compatibility. Existing liners are outside this phase. The M5 test
uses the **complete production cap**, which can be reused with a later M5 full
body, plus a 113.6 mm cropped body. Its 125.6 mm assembled length and full guide,
roots, controls and receivers represent the revised joint. It does not reproduce
the omitted lower body's flexibility or remote handling loads.

## What was consuming space

M4's closed protective liners continued from below the spring roots all the way
to Z252, the end of the 78 mm sleeve. The springs themselves end at Z182.4. The
long empty continuation therefore narrowed the storage space despite having no
spring to protect. Simply shortening those guards to Z190 gives about **11 mL**
without changing the springs or the exterior.

M5 retains the liners beside the complete moving springs, ending 7.6 mm beyond
their tips. Above them, a continuous **1.6 mm stepped guide ring** replaces the
two bulky guard boxes. The ring's longitudinal reliefs are necessary: the
reinforced cap receivers must slide past it during the entire withdrawal. Removing
those reliefs would create a roomier drawing but a cap that binds on removal.

The guide is moved toward the perimeter where possible. Its broad-face depth
and 78 mm overlap remain; the cap's 2.7 mm broad-face walls remain through the
bearing region. The catches remain at the same world positions with exactly the
same leaf geometry. M4's raised reinforcing strips become part of the flat outer
wall, retaining their internal receiver faces and pocket skin. The complete old
receiver-strip region is preserved by actual solid comparison, not a nominal
thickness assertion.

## Alternatives and the reason for selection

[Raw measured sections, volume and stiffness quantities](m5/space_study.json)

| Actual CAD alternative | Central area at Z220 | Individual tube passage | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M4 reference | 2614 mm² | .95 mm minimum gap | Historical unprinted baseline |
| Shorten guard only | 2794 mm² | .95 mm | Useful +11 mL, retains raised exterior ribs |
| Short guard + 1.2 mm sleeve | 2880 mm² | .95 mm | Rejected: same-material local plate rigidity falls to 42% of the 1.6 mm wall |
| Flat 75 mm shell | 2781 mm² | Intersects the 55 × 40 mm tube envelope | Rejected: inward catches make the entrance worse |
| Flat 77 mm, thinner broad cap faces | 2961 mm² | .95 mm | Avoided: cap-section bending inertia falls about 21% / 13% on its two axes |
| Flat 77 mm, mismatched corner profiles | 2863 mm² | .95 mm | Rejected by adverse diagonal motion, despite satisfactory principal-plane results |
| **M5: flat 77 mm, retained broad-face thickness** | **2866 mm²** | **.95 mm** | Selected compromise |

The selected design gives back less space than the thinner cap, deliberately.
At Z220, its guide bending inertias are about **4% / 15% higher** than M4's;
its cap inertias are about **2% / 5% lower**. These are actual section integrals
for straight-beam comparison under equal effective material, not a shell-contact
simulation or wall-spreading rating. Local receiver geometry and wall thickness
are preserved. The simpler 1.2 mm sleeve alternative would reduce local plate
rigidity by much more than this, so it was not chosen.

At the central guide section the clear width grows from **56.9 to 66.1 mm**.
The **minimum catch-level width stays 56.9 mm**, including the same .95 mm gap
to the existing individual toothpaste envelope. The extra upper space helps
objects occupy and move within the case; it does not let an object wider than
the entrance pass through. Combined real brush/tube packing, alternative tubes
and comfortable access remain physical unknowns. No thinner or tapered toothpaste
proxy was invented to make a candidate fit.

## How the space measurement was checked

The study slices the actual body and cap with a .02 mm slab and selects the
void containing the case axis. Hidden disconnected catch channels and the
guide/cap clearance are excluded. Counting all empty CAD volume would incorrectly
call those mechanism spaces storage. A synthetic disconnected-channel regression
protects this distinction.

Midpoint integration along the interior gives M4 **800.86 mL** and M5
**833.83 mL** at 2 mm station spacing: a **32.97 mL / 4.1%** increase. At 4 mm
spacing the increase also rounds to 32.97 mL. The individual totals move by about 1 mL under
this refinement; the comparison changes by about .002 mL. These are consistent
approximate central-void estimates, not calibrated liquid capacity or a proof
of simultaneous packing. Of the improvement, about 11 mL is demonstrated by the
unchanged-exterior short-guard control; the remainder combines perimeter changes
and the wider flat broad body.

## Retention, release and what transfers

[Current CAD evidence](m5/cad.json) covers 273 poses and specific structural
connectivity checks for the new guard/ring junction and receiver strips. It checks
the established brush and tube passage, actual end rounds, individual flat capture
on both catches, the leaf's free space, seated clearance, deliberately released
withdrawal through the full guide and control return after the teeth clear.
The matching sample is checked against production body/cap geometry.

The leaf's symmetric difference against M4 is zero. Both catches retain
**14.4 mm²** of actual flat shoulder land. The old isolated 7 N/E1200 pressure
fixture therefore retains its narrow scope: local leaf deflection under its
original fixed-root/pad loading. This does not predict full-cap release force,
printed comfort, recovery or holding force. No new native holding simulation or
calibrated material result is claimed.

[Updated contact study](../m5_contacts.py) and [witnesses](m5/contact.json) retain
both principal rocking planes, nine side-gap/axial-play combinations and nominal
and adverse diagonal paths. Nominal guide bearing still occurs before catch
pickup. The shortened 30 mm guide control again loads a catch before guide support.
The first M5 corner arrangement passed both nominal principal paths but failed
the adverse diagonal comparison: it demanded about .112 mm catch rise before
bearing, exceeding the inherited .056 mm adverse local proxy. This was caught by
the implementation's regression before final review. The rejected geometry remains
reproducible as `flat77_loose_corners`, with an explicit rejected contact witness.
The corrected cap cavity and sleeve use concentric corner profiles with the same
declared bearing clearance. The [corner drawing](m5/corner_bearing.svg) explains
why flat-face clearance alone missed this route. This is an actual geometry
correction; the old proxy limit was not relaxed to make the result pass.

The .20 mm gap/.35 mm axial-play corner still loads a catch slightly first;
its small displacement is compared with the already declared M4 adverse local
proxy, not silently promoted to a physical holding-force bound. Finite prescribed
paths do not search all 3D poses or include guide deformation.

Printed PETG anisotropy, creep, shell flexure, dimensional errors and bridge finish
remain uncalibrated. The section comparison uses equal material so its geometric
ratios do not depend on choosing one favorable modulus, but it cannot establish
physical retention. The printed failed M1 remains the authoritative negative
reference. M4 was also unprinted; no M4 physical success transfers to M5.

## Manufacturing and use

Use the same intended PETG / .4 mm nozzle / .2 mm layer starting setup. The
reference slice uses two walls and 7% adaptive cubic as a diagnostic. Body upright,
cap closed-end down; paired exports share this placement. The full body remains
252 mm high within the selected Q2C's 256 mm height. The body/test roots, printing
direction and rounded bed-contact foot are retained. The test's cut annular body
end is its bed contact.

The 1.6 mm guide, 2 mm leaf and 1.2 mm guard liner are retained. Guard termination
leaves an exposed internal end but introduces no roof over the spring. The new
stepped guide starts against existing guard sidewalls; its cross-section changes
need the current slice/bridge inspection. Do not infer unsupported print quality
from solid connectivity or a successful export.

Both current diagnostic slices complete without notices. The inspected actual
[full](m5/case_toolpaths.svg) and [sample](m5/joint_toolpaths.svg) toolpaths show
7.31 mm body tooth bridges (Z178.2 full / Z39.8 sample) and 13.06 mm cap bridges
at Z86.6. The automatic-support probe proposes interfaces inside the cap near its
floor (Z2.4–2.6) and receiving ledges (Z86.0–86.2), as in M4. No extra bridge role
is reported at the shortened-guard/stepped-guide transition. Primary paths use
no supports; actual bridge finish and safe support removal remain unmeasured.
This is diagnostic slice acceptance, not support-free qualification or Orca GUI
STEP-import verification.

Operation remains: push fully home and allow both controls to return. Unload the
joint, press both controls, withdraw the first 10 mm until the cap mouth clears
the teeth, release the controls, then slide off the remaining guide. The new test
should be compared with controls untouched and deliberately pressed; a click alone
is insufficient. Binding, incomplete return, whitening or easy unpressed separation
is evidence for correction. No new print has been requested.

Keeping the same spring and pad while making the broad body flat at 77 mm also
recesses the pad 1 mm farther behind that broad outer face. The open control port
and prescribed travel remain clear; printed finger access and comfort need the
new sample rather than inheriting a tactile result from an unprinted revision.

Final independent engineering review inspected the actual source, all four
drawings, section/contact evidence and matching publication/slice records. It
found no critical defect requiring correction before delivery as a space-recovery
prototype. The comparison remains conditional on printed fit, guide/shell behavior,
bridge quality and recovery; no travel-retention rating follows from the review.
