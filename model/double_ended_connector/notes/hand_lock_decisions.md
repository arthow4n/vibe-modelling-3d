# All-printed hand locks — revised brief and concept screen

## User correction and authorized phase

The user rejects the interpretation that metal hardware is wanted: ditch other
materials for the connector, use all printed components. It must open smartly by
hand and not shake off easily. They request several SVG mechanism proposals
again, not detailed CAD. Earlier A/B/C are retained as historical evidence of the
misunderstanding, not a set to print. The existing attached key ring may of course
be metal; no supplied connector component is metal.

Retain independent ends, no need to rotate the attachments, PLA/PETG suitability
without spring-return gates, useful bending or friction-fit clips, generic sizing
chosen autonomously. New sketches deliberately avoid loose pieces during daily
opening and tool-operated keepers. Ordinary threaded contact/friction is not
excluded, but unmeasured thread friction cannot be called a shake-proof lock.
No new dependency on spool elasticity is introduced.

## Why three different arrangements

An unclosed hook cannot meet retention intent. A bare quarter-turn bayonet with
no spring/preload or secondary lock has a reverse route under arbitrary motion;
a rigid push/turn sequence by itself is not evidence it remains parked when
unloaded. It was not selected over the simpler multi-turn concepts. Elastic
snap gates were excluded for the original rigid-mechanism requirement. No
mechanism research, material calibration or force analysis is claimed here.
These are original schematic proposals, using familiar rigid screw/guide/rotor
relationships, not adaptations of third-party drawings.

D replaces the entire gate with a threaded barrel that bridges the mouth and
cups the upper horn. A lower threaded stem supports it. Barrel cavities are
stepped: a broad smooth upper bore clears the stop tip, a smaller lower threaded
portion does not pass it. Install the tip after threading on the barrel; base
shoulder limits retraction, tip limits overrun. The proposed assembly is five
parts including both tips. Threaded stem / cavity / tip packaging must be resolved
in CAD; the sketch is not proof of the assembly route. Target about 6 mm exposed
mouth, about 2 mm nose overlap and several turns before opening. Actual usable
thread lead is deliberately undecided until stroke and self-locking behavior can
be screened. D is recommended for simplicity, not a measured security advantage.

E has a nonrotating guided bolt at each mouth, a threaded bore inside that bolt
and an axially captured lead screw. A shoulder on the thumbwheel shaft is trapped
by the body and a shared screwed-on cover, so turning the wheel translates the
bolt rather than withdrawing the screw. Guide faces prevent bolt rotation; an
opposite socket supports its closed nose. Rail/end stops are to keep the bolt
from winding completely out. Eight proposed parts: body, cover, two bolts, two
lead screws, two printed assembly screws. Approximately 7–8 mm stroke (mouth plus
nose engagement) requires a longer housing; both bolts must clear one another
through their full travel. The icon is a simplified face view; enlarged section
shows the thread and bearing relationship. Thread back-drive, assembly-screw
back-out and dust remain unqualified.

F has two rigid rotors between body and cover cheeks, turning on integral
shoulder posts. The gate covers the mouth in its closed orientation, seated
against a horn shoulder. A captive threaded thumb lock crosses a hole in the
rotor and enters a clearance pocket below; it blocks rotation without depending
on clamp force. The lock head/reduced neck is captured by the cheeks, with enough
axial lost travel to withdraw the tip completely. The user then rotates the gate
90 degrees. The control sits below the pivot, so its lobe swings sideways rather
than leaving an upward heel in the open mouth. Eight proposed parts: body,
cover, two rotors, two locks, two printed cover screws. Simultaneous capture,
thread engagement and tip clearance need CAD; no snap assembly is assumed.
A back-out lock does not immediately uncover the mouth, but neither independent
motion nor a threaded lock proves vibration resistance.

## Whole-object screen and finish line

Target overall lengths ~44–48 mm, widths22–24 mm and thicknesses10–11 mm,
including controls. These are schematic envelope targets, not measured CAD.
Free horns target ~3.2 × 4 mm section (nominal diagonal ~5.1 mm) against the
cord's 7.6 mm across-flats opening. This rough size screen motivates a slim horn
with larger lock housing near the center; it does not establish the actual
angled loading path or clearance around the loop. The existing ring wire target
is ≤3 mm. Open mouths target ~6 mm. Generic sizing remains delegated.

Two hands are assumed: one stabilizes the body, the other operates the chosen
end. The other end stays closed; no attached object spins with the control.
All parts are printed separately and assembled without elastic snaps. Ordinary
assembly screws remain printed and hand-accessible, with no included printed
tool. Small fastener size cannot simply copy an M3 metal screw; choose coarse,
solid-core nozzle-appropriate threads if detailed development proceeds.

The useful finish line for this phase is a clear comparison plus sectioned
holding/release explanations. Four generated SVGs were parsed as XML, rendered
with CairoSVG and opened for visual review. Review found that the first F rotor
heel intruded into the mouth in the open view; the lock lobe was moved below the
pivot before delivery. The barrel section now explicitly distinguishes its wide
stop-tip cavity and narrower threaded skirt. No CAD fit, motion verification,
load, slice or shaking result follows from these schematic corrections.

The next useful investment after discussion is one selected complete prototype:
actual attachment loading path, all motion/assembly/capture relationships,
coarse threads, end seats and two-end clearance before exporting a print. The
physical trial should include unloaded shaking, rubbing/twisting with keys,
deliberate opening, wear and modest representative pull. No numeric load or
shake-resistance acceptance criterion was supplied in this SVG phase.

## Reflection

The initial permission to use available hardware was over-weighted relative to
the desired everyday hand interaction. The correction is recorded in the object
and in scoped user preferences, overriding the earlier allowance. The governing
skill already requires the whole operating sequence and assembly burden to be
reviewed; another global process rule is unnecessary. The concrete change is
all-printed captive hand controls instead of tools and parts removed on every
opening. Remaining uncertainty belongs to the chosen design's physical trial.


## Sequential CAD authorization and first increment

The user now explicitly authorizes modelling D, E and F one after another, with
assembled pictures as well as separated parts. They request incremental progress
so quota interruption does not lose everything. D is the first saved increment;
E then F remain authorized next work. No further concept-selection approval is
needed merely to model those named mechanisms. Earlier SVG-only phase limits
above describe the historical handoff, not the current authorization.

The first D construction exposed two assembly mistakes before delivery: a sleeve
cannot be forced onto an already captured integral stem; even a split integral
stem crosses the closed annular sleeve wall on frame closure. Separate keyed
stems fix this: thread on each barrel axially, then capture each stem/barrel unit
between the two frame halves. Eight printed parts result. The frame must close
with the barrels retracted, since closed barrels cup the fixed noses; a check
requiring assembly with the barrels closed was corrected to match that explicit
sequence. The native approach/motion checks pass for that prescribed path.

The CAD model is larger than the schematic to accommodate real screw travel and
keyed-root capture: approximately 62 mm long with 10.8 mm barrels. The two-mm
pitch gives four turns over eight mm of travel, a six-mm nominal open mouth and
two-mm closed nose overlap. These are design values, not physical measurements.
The [D record](d_model.md) owns assembly instructions and current evidence.
